I studied Industrial Engineering because I liked the idea of making systems work better — fewer bottlenecks, less waste, clearer processes. What I didn't expect was how directly that translates into data work.

At Rappi, most of my day is spent moving between SQL, spreadsheets, and dashboards — but the underlying questions are the same ones I learned to ask in school: where is time being lost, what's the real constraint, and how do we know if a change actually worked?

A few things I keep coming back to:

- **Data is just a faster feedback loop for operations.** A weekly performance report is a process improvement tool, not just a report.
- **Automation is the natural next step.** Once you've done a manual analysis a few times, it's worth asking whether it should run itself.
- **The best insight is the one someone acts on.** A dashboard nobody opens is worse than no dashboard at all.

This site is partly a portfolio and partly a place to think out loud about this intersection of operations, data, and automation — plus the occasional post about training, since the same "track it, test it, adjust it" mindset shows up there too.

More posts (and projects) coming as I build them.
